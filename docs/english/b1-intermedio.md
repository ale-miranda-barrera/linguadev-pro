> **Tiempo estimado para dominar el nivel:** 12-16 semanas con 60 min/día. Es el nivel más largo del recorrido A2→B2 por la cantidad de gramática nueva (tiempos perfectos, condicionales, pasiva, reported speech, modales de deducción).

## 0. Antes de empezar

### Diagnóstico — ¿es B1 tu nivel actual?

B1 es el nivel oficial de "usuario independiente" según el CEFR. Marca las casillas:

- ✅/❌ Traducir sin dudar: *"If I had more time, I would learn Japanese, but I'm too busy at the moment."*
- ✅/❌ Usar reported speech: *"She said (that) she had been working late every night that week."*
- ✅/❌ Convertir voz activa en pasiva: *"The company launched a new product last month → A new product was launched by the company last month."*
- ✅/❌ Distinguir *must/have to/should* con seguridad (obligación externa vs interna vs consejo).
- ✅/❌ Formar oraciones con *present perfect continuous* correctamente (*"I've been studying English for 5 years"*).
- ✅/❌ Explicar la diferencia entre *"If I win the lottery"* y *"If I won the lottery"*.
- ✅/❌ Usar 10+ phrasal verbs de trabajo o negocios sin pensar (*take on, carry out, come up with, put forward, follow up*).
- ✅/❌ Leer un artículo de la BBC (news) sin diccionario y captar 80%+ de la idea principal.

Si marcaste 6+ → estás en B1. Si marcaste 8 → salta a B1+. Si menos de 5 → vuelve a `a2plus-consolidacion.md`.

B1 es donde el estudio empieza a parecerse a **inmersión estructurada**. Consumir contenido nativo con propósito es tan importante como estudiar gramática.

**Frecuencia mínima recomendada:** 60 min/día. Menos, y el volumen de contenido nuevo (5 tiempos verbales, condicionales, pasiva, reported speech, modales de deducción) no se consolida.

## 1. Descripción del nivel CEFR B1

B1 es el **umbral (Threshold)** según el CEFR — el punto en que dejas de ser "usuario elemental" y pasas a ser "usuario independiente". El Council of Europe lo describe así: *"Es capaz de comprender los puntos principales de textos claros y en lengua estándar si tratan sobre cuestiones que le son conocidas, ya sea en situaciones de trabajo, de estudio o de ocio. Sabe desenvolverse en la mayor parte de las situaciones que pueden surgir durante un viaje por zonas donde se utiliza la lengua. Es capaz de producir textos sencillos y coherentes sobre temas que le son familiares o en los que tiene un interés personal. Puede describir experiencias, acontecimientos, deseos y aspiraciones, así como justificar brevemente sus opiniones o explicar sus planes."*

Este es el nivel de **autonomía funcional**. Un B1 puede vivir en un país anglófono sin traductor: puede alquilar un piso, resolver problemas con el banco, ir al médico y explicar síntomas complejos, entender la mayoría de las series con subtítulos en inglés, mantener una conversación de 30 minutos sobre temas cotidianos con un desconocido, y leer artículos de prensa de complejidad media sin buscar más de 5-6 palabras por artículo.

Los **can-do statements oficiales B1** (CEFR Companion Volume 2020) son:

- **Comprensión oral:** entiende la información principal de programas de radio o TV sobre asuntos actuales o de interés personal cuando la articulación es relativamente lenta y clara; sigue conferencias o charlas sobre temas familiares si la estructura es clara.
- **Comprensión lectora:** lee textos en los que predomina un lenguaje cotidiano o relacionado con su trabajo; entiende la descripción de acontecimientos, sentimientos y deseos en cartas personales.
- **Expresión oral (interacción):** se desenvuelve en casi todas las situaciones que se le presentan cuando viaja; participa espontáneamente en conversaciones sobre temas cotidianos, personales o de actualidad.
- **Expresión oral (producción):** enlaza frases de forma sencilla para describir experiencias y hechos, sus sueños, esperanzas y ambiciones; explica y justifica brevemente sus opiniones y proyectos; narra una historia o la trama de un libro o película.
- **Expresión escrita:** escribe textos sencillos y bien enlazados sobre temas conocidos; escribe cartas personales describiendo experiencias e impresiones.

En términos de exámenes oficiales, B1 corresponde a: **Cambridge English B1 Preliminary (PET)**, IELTS 4.0-5.0, TOEFL iBT 42-71, TOEIC 550-780, Aptis nivel B1. **PET es el examen de referencia** — apuntar al 70% (Pass) al final del nivel es el objetivo.

**Qué NO debes esperar dominar en B1.** No manejarás con fluidez el tercer condicional ("If I had known, I would have called"), que es contenido central de B2. No dominarás sarcasmo, ironía o humor sutil. No podrás leer novelas literarias sin ayuda constante (Murakami traducido: sí; Ian McEwan original: no aún). No podrás participar en debates académicos ni sostener conversaciones profesionales de negociación compleja. No entenderás dialectos regionales fuertes (cockney, Glaswegian, deep Southern US). Todo eso llega en B2/C1.

Al terminar B1 tu vocabulario activo estará en el rango **2.500-3.500 palabras**, con pasivo de 5.000-6.000. Un lector B1 competente puede leer un artículo de la BBC de 800 palabras y entender el 90%+ sin ayuda; puede ver un capítulo de Friends con subtítulos en inglés y captar 75%+; puede escribir un ensayo de 250-300 palabras con estructura clara sobre un tema conocido.

**El "efecto meseta" de B1.** Muchos estudiantes se estancan en B1 durante años. La razón: los packs de gramática se acaban, pero el vocabulario y la fluidez requieren volumen de exposición que la mayoría no acumula. La única salida es cambiar la relación con la lengua: pasar de estudiante a usuario. Consumir 30 min diarios de contenido nativo durante 6-12 meses acelera B1 → B2 más que cualquier libro.

## 2. Gramática core del nivel B1

En B1 la gramática nueva son **siete pilares**: present perfect vs past simple (dominio), present perfect continuous, past perfect, second conditional, reported speech, passive voice y modales avanzados (must have / can't have / should have). Además consolidas relative clauses, gerunds/infinitives y phrasal verbs.

### 2.1 Present perfect vs past simple — el gran examen de B1

Este es el punto de gramática que separa B1 de A2+. Ambos hablan del pasado, pero desde ángulos distintos.

**Past simple:** acción **completa** en un momento específico del pasado. Hay conexión con "cuándo" (aunque no se diga).

**Present perfect:** acción con **relevancia en el presente**. No importa cuándo, importa el resultado o la experiencia.

Tabla comparativa (memoriza esta tabla):

| Situación | Past simple | Present perfect |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| Tiempo específico | *I saw him yesterday.* | ❌ *"I've seen him yesterday"* |
| Sin tiempo específico | ❌ *"I saw him at some point in my life"* → mejor: | *I have seen him before.* |
| Experiencia de vida | ❌ | *Have you ever been to Japan?* |
| Cambio reciente | *He got a new job last month.* | *He has just got a new job.* (más reciente) |
| Situación que continúa | ❌ | *I have lived here for 10 years.* (y sigo aquí) |
| Situación completa | *I lived in Paris for 5 years.* (ya no vivo allí) | ❌ |
| Duración con *since/for* | ❌ | *She has worked here since 2020.* |

**Marcadores temporales típicos:**
- **Past simple:** *yesterday, last week/month/year, in 2020, ago, when I was young, in my childhood*.
- **Present perfect:** *ever, never, already, yet, just, so far, up to now, in my life, this week/month* (aún no terminado), *since 2020, for 5 years*.

**Ejemplos comparados:**
- *I've been to Rome twice.* (experiencia de vida, no importa cuándo) vs *I went to Rome in 2019.* (tiempo específico).
- *She has finished the report.* (resultado presente: está listo) vs *She finished the report at 5 pm.* (evento en el pasado).
- *Have you seen the new movie?* (¿ya en tu vida?) vs *Did you see the new movie last night?* (evento concreto).

**Regla mental operativa:** *"si puedo/quiero decir CUÁNDO exactamente, uso past simple; si el punto es la experiencia o el resultado ahora, present perfect."*

**Error típico #1 hispanohablante:** traducir el pretérito perfecto español literalmente ("he visto") como present perfect siempre. En inglés americano moderno, *"I already ate"* es más común que *"I've already eaten"*. En británico se mantiene más el present perfect.

**Error típico #2:** usar present perfect con *ago*. *"I've seen him 5 minutes ago"* ❌ → *"I saw him 5 minutes ago"* ✅. *Ago* siempre pide past simple.

### 2.2 Present perfect continuous — foco en la duración

**Regla:** *have/has + been + verbo-ing*. Se usa para acciones que empezaron en el pasado y (a) continúan hasta ahora, con foco en el proceso o duración, o (b) acaban de terminar con evidencia visible.

Ejemplos uso a — acción que sigue:
- *I have been studying English for 3 years.* → He estado estudiando inglés durante 3 años (y sigo).
- *She has been working on this project since January.*
- *They have been living together for 6 months.*
- *How long have you been waiting?*

Ejemplos uso b — acabada con evidencia:
- *You look exhausted. Have you been running?*
- *I've been cooking all afternoon; that's why the kitchen is a mess.*
- *Her eyes are red. She's been crying.*

**Diferencia con present perfect simple:**

| Present perfect simple | Present perfect continuous |
|-----------------------|---------------------------|
| Foco en **resultado o cantidad completa** | Foco en **duración o proceso** |
| *I have read 3 books this month.* | *I have been reading a lot lately.* |
| *She has written the report.* (terminado) | *She has been writing the report all morning.* (proceso) |
| *He has painted the wall.* (terminado) | *He has been painting the wall.* (aún puede estar en ello) |

**Verbos que NO se usan en continuous (stative verbs):** *know, believe, understand, love, hate, want, need, own, belong* — con estos usa siempre present perfect simple.

**Error típico:** confundir *"I've been working"* con *"I've worked"*. La diferencia es sutil pero importante: *"I've worked in three companies"* (cuántas — resultado) vs *"I've been working in this company for 5 years"* (duración de una).

### 2.3 Past perfect — el pasado del pasado

**Regla:** *had + past participle*. Se usa para hablar de una acción que ocurrió **antes de otro momento pasado**. Es el "pasado del pasado".

Ejemplos:
- *When I arrived at the station, the train had already left.* → Cuando llegué a la estación, el tren ya se había ido.
- *She was tired because she had worked 12 hours.* → Estaba cansada porque había trabajado 12 horas.
- *By 2020, they had lived in five different countries.*
- *I didn't recognize him because I hadn't seen him in years.*
- *After she had finished her homework, she watched a movie.*

**Diagrama temporal:**

```
Past perfect        Past simple         Now
(had done)          (did)              (present)
    |                   |                 |
    v                   v                 v
    A -------- (before) B <-------------- |
```

**Usos típicos:**

1. **Narrativa clara del orden temporal.** *"When I got home, my sister had already eaten dinner."* (Primero cenó, luego llegué).
2. **Con verbos de percepción o recuerdo pasado.** *"I realized I had left my keys at the office."*
3. **Reported speech** (ver §2.5): *"He said he had never seen anything like it."*
4. **Tercer condicional** — anticipo de B2 (ver §2.6).

**Sin past perfect (con past simple secuencial):**

Si el orden ya está claro con conectores como *before, after, when + first*, el past perfect es opcional:
- *When I arrived, the train left.* (llegó y luego salió — same time)
- *When I arrived, the train had left.* (llegó y ya se había ido — different)
- *After she finished dinner, she went out.* ✅ (orden claro por *after*)
- *After she had finished dinner, she went out.* ✅ (también correcto, énfasis en la anterioridad)

**Error típico:** usarlo cuando basta past simple. *"Yesterday I had gone to the gym"* ❌ (no hay referencia a otro pasado) → *"Yesterday I went to the gym"* ✅.

### 2.4 Second conditional — situaciones hipotéticas presentes

**Regla:** *If + past simple, would + verbo base*.
Se usa para (a) situaciones imaginarias/hipotéticas en el presente o futuro, y (b) consejos indirectos (*"If I were you, I would…"*).

Ejemplos:
- *If I had more money, I would buy a house.* → Si tuviera más dinero, compraría una casa. (No tengo el dinero.)
- *If she spoke French, she would apply for that job.* → No lo habla.
- *What would you do if you won the lottery?* → Hipotético clásico.
- *I wouldn't say that if I were you.* → Consejo indirecto (siempre *were*, no *was*, en frases con *if I were*).

**Diferencia crucial vs first conditional:**

| First conditional | Second conditional |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| Situación **real/posible** en el futuro. | Situación **imaginaria/improbable** en presente/futuro. |
| *If it rains, I'll stay home.* (probable que llueva) | *If it snowed in Miami, people would panic.* (improbable). |
| *If you study, you'll pass.* (consejo realista) | *If I were president, I would change everything.* (hipotético). |

**Fórmula memorable para consejos:** *"If I were you, I would…"* (nunca *was*).

**Error típico:** usar *would* en la cláusula del *if*. *"If I would have more money…"* ❌ → *"If I had more money…"* ✅. El *if* del segundo condicional nunca lleva *would*.

### 2.5 Reported speech — contar lo que alguien dijo

**Regla:** cuando el verbo introductorio está en pasado (*said, told, asked, explained*), los tiempos verbales del discurso original retroceden un paso ("backshift").

Tabla de backshift:

| Discurso directo | Reported speech |
|------------------|-----------------|
| *"I am tired"* → present simple | *He said (that) he was tired.* → past simple |
| *"I'm cooking"* → present continuous | *He said he was cooking.* → past continuous |
| *"I saw her"* → past simple | *He said he had seen her.* → past perfect |
| *"I've finished"* → present perfect | *He said he had finished.* → past perfect |
| *"I'll call"* → will | *He said he would call.* → would |
| *"I can swim"* → can | *He said he could swim.* → could |
| *"I must go"* → must | *He said he had to go.* → had to |

**También cambian los referentes temporales/espaciales:**

| Directo | Reported |
|---------|----------|
| today | that day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| now | then / at that moment |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| next week | the following week |
| ago | before |

**Ejemplos completos:**
- Direct: *"I'm going to the party tomorrow."* → Reported: *She said she was going to the party the next day.*
- Direct: *"I saw him yesterday."* → Reported: *He said he had seen him the day before.*
- Direct: *"I'll finish it later."* → Reported: *She promised she would finish it later.*

**Reported questions:** cambian a estructura de afirmación (sin inversión) y usan *if/whether* para yes/no questions.

- Direct: *"Where do you live?"* → Reported: *He asked where I lived.*
- Direct: *"Are you tired?"* → Reported: *She asked if/whether I was tired.*
- Direct: *"What time is it?"* → Reported: *He asked what time it was.*

**Reported commands/requests:** *told/asked + persona + to + infinitivo*.

- Direct: *"Close the door."* → Reported: *He told me to close the door.*
- Direct: *"Please don't smoke here."* → Reported: *She asked him not to smoke there.*

**Cuándo NO haces backshift:** si lo dicho sigue siendo verdad en el momento de reportar y el contexto es reciente, muchos hablantes mantienen el tiempo original:
- *She said she is a doctor.* (aún lo es, es reciente)
- *He said the earth is round.* (verdad universal)

**Error típico hispanohablante:** usar *"He said me…"* ❌. En inglés:
- *say* NO acepta objeto persona directo. Correcto: *"He said (that) he was tired"* / *"He said TO me…"* (con "to").
- *tell* SÍ acepta objeto persona. Correcto: *"He told me (that) he was tired"*.

**Packs:** `b1/10-reported-speech-statements`, `b1/11-reported-speech-questions`, `b1/12-reported-commands-requests`.

### 2.6 Passive voice — el foco cambia al objeto

**Regla:** *[objeto de la activa] + be + past participle + (by agente)*. Se usa cuando (a) el agente no es importante o es desconocido, (b) el foco está en la acción o el receptor, (c) contextos formales/científicos/noticias.

Formación en cada tiempo:

| Tiempo | Activa | Pasiva |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Present simple | *They make cars in Germany.* | *Cars are made in Germany.* |
| Present continuous | *They are building a bridge.* | *A bridge is being built.* |
| Past simple | *He wrote the book in 2020.* | *The book was written in 2020.* |
| Past continuous | *They were repairing the road.* | *The road was being repaired.* |
| Present perfect | *Someone has stolen my bike.* | *My bike has been stolen.* |
| Past perfect | *They had cancelled the meeting.* | *The meeting had been cancelled.* |
| Future will | *They will announce the winner.* | *The winner will be announced.* |
| Modal | *They can fix it.* | *It can be fixed.* |
| Modal perfect | *Someone must have taken it.* | *It must have been taken.* |

**Ejemplos en contexto:**
- *The Mona Lisa was painted by Leonardo da Vinci in the early 16th century.*
- *English is spoken in over 60 countries.*
- *A cure for cancer has not been found yet.*
- *This building was designed by Frank Lloyd Wright.*
- *The report will be published next month.*
- *My wallet was stolen on the train.*

**Cuándo NO usar pasiva:**
- Cuando el agente es importante y da información. Nunca *"The Nobel Prize was won by someone"* — mejor decir por quién si importa.
- En estilo informal narrativo. Preferimos activa por defecto: *"John broke the vase"* > *"The vase was broken by John"*.
- Cuando el objeto se puede omitir sin pérdida: *"They are having a meeting"* (activa) es mejor que *"A meeting is being had"* (raro).

**Formalidad:** la pasiva es más común en inglés formal: reportajes, artículos científicos, avisos oficiales, procesos técnicos.

**Error típico hispanohablante:** abusar del *"se + verbo"* español traducido literal. *"Se hicieron los cambios"* ≠ *"The changes are made themselves"* ❌ → *"The changes were made"* ✅.

**Passive with two objects:** verbos como *give, send, tell, offer, show, teach* pueden formar dos pasivas:
- Active: *They gave me a prize.*
- Passive 1 (persona-focus): *I was given a prize.*
- Passive 2 (cosa-focus): *A prize was given to me.*

**Packs:** `b1/08-passive-present-past`, `b1/09-passive-modals`.

### 2.7 Modales avanzados (must have, might have, can't have, should have)

**Modales de deducción en el presente:**

- **must** — deducción positiva casi segura. *"She's not answering. She must be busy."*
- **might/may/could** — deducción posible. *"He might be at the gym."*
- **can't** — deducción negativa segura. *"That can't be right, let me check."*

**Modales de deducción en el pasado (must have + past participle):**

- **must have + PP** — deducción positiva pasada. *"The ground is wet. It must have rained."*
- **might/could have + PP** — deducción posible pasada. *"He didn't answer. He might have been busy."*
- **can't/couldn't have + PP** — imposibilidad pasada. *"She can't have said that! It doesn't sound like her."*

Ejemplos:
- *You must have been so tired after that long trip.*
- *I might have left my keys in the car.*
- *They can't have finished already — the project just started.*
- *She could have called me if she needed help.*

**Modales de arrepentimiento/consejo pasado (should have):**

- **should have + PP** — arrepentimiento o crítica retrospectiva. *"I should have studied more for the exam."*
- **shouldn't have + PP** — algo que hicimos y no debíamos. *"You shouldn't have eaten that; you knew you were allergic."*

Ejemplos:
- *I should have listened to you.*
- *We shouldn't have gone out in that storm.*
- *You should have told me earlier.*

**Obligación pasada:**

- **had to** — obligación cumplida en el pasado. *"I had to work last weekend."*
- **didn't have to** — no era necesario, no lo hice. *"I didn't have to pay; it was free."*
- **needn't have + PP** — no era necesario, pero LO HICE. *"You needn't have brought a gift; it wasn't required."* (Trajiste el regalo pero no era necesario).

**Packs:** `b1/16-modals-deduction-present`, `b1/17-modals-deduction-past`, `b1/18-modals-obligation-past`.

### 2.8 Relative clauses avanzadas (defining vs non-defining)

**Defining relative clauses** (sin comas, no separables — dan información esencial):
- *The book (that) I bought yesterday is really good.* → Necesaria para saber qué libro.
- *The man who lives next door is a doctor.*

**Non-defining relative clauses** (con comas, información extra removible):
- *My brother, who lives in Berlin, is a doctor.* → Solo tengo un hermano; el dato es extra.
- *This book, which I bought in Paris, is my favorite.*

**Diferencia crítica:**

| Frase | Interpretación |
|-------|---------------|
| *"My sister who lives in Madrid is a doctor."* | Tengo varias hermanas; la de Madrid es la doctora. |
| *"My sister, who lives in Madrid, is a doctor."* | Solo tengo una hermana, y vive en Madrid. |

**Reglas de uso:**
- En defining clauses puedes usar *that* en lugar de *who/which*.
- En non-defining clauses NUNCA usas *that* — obligatorio *who/which*.
- En defining clauses puedes omitir el relativo si es objeto: *"The book (that) I bought"* — el "that" es opcional.
- En non-defining clauses NUNCA omites el relativo.

**Ejemplos avanzados:**
- *The house whose roof is red belongs to my grandmother.* → *whose* para posesión.
- *This is the town where I grew up.* → *where* para lugar.
- *That was the year when everything changed.* → *when* para tiempo.
- *The reason why he left is unclear.* → *why* para razón.

**Packs:** `b1/13-relative-clauses-defining`, `b1/14-relative-clauses-non-defining`.

### 2.9 Gerunds vs infinitives — dominio

Extensión de A2+ con más verbos y más categorías:

**Verbos que aceptan ambos con significado similar:** *love, like, hate, prefer, start, begin, continue*.
- *I love reading / I love to read.* → prácticamente iguales.

**Verbos que aceptan ambos con significado distinto:** *stop, remember, forget, try, regret, mean, need, go on*.
- *Stop smoking* (dejar de fumar) vs *Stop to smoke* (parar para fumar).
- *Remember calling her* (recordar que la llamé) vs *Remember to call her* (recordar hacerlo).
- *I regret telling him* (me arrepiento de haberle dicho) vs *I regret to tell you* (lamento tener que decirte).
- *The car needs washing* (= needs to be washed).
- *He went on talking for hours* (continuó hablando) vs *He went on to become a doctor* (después llegó a ser).

**Verbos + objeto + infinitivo:** *want, need, expect, allow, tell, ask, help, teach*.
- *I want you to help me.*
- *She told him to leave.*
- *They allowed us to enter.*

**Después de *make/let* — infinitivo sin *to*:**
- *He made me laugh.*
- *Let me go.*
- *My parents wouldn't let me stay out late.*

**Pack:** `b1/15-gerunds-vs-infinitives-advanced`.

### 2.10 Future continuous y future perfect

**Future continuous:** *will be + verbo-ing*. Acción en curso en un momento del futuro.
- *This time tomorrow, I'll be flying to Tokyo.*
- *At 8 pm tonight, we'll be having dinner.*
- *Don't call at 3; I'll be sleeping.*

**Future perfect:** *will have + past participle*. Acción completada antes de un momento del futuro.
- *By 2030, I will have graduated.*
- *She'll have finished the report by 5 pm.*
- *By the time you arrive, we'll have left.*

**Packs:** `b1/20-future-continuous`, `b1/21-future-perfect`.

## 4. Listening — patrones a dominar en B1

### 4.1 Velocidad y tipos

B1 te expone a audios de **130-150 wpm** — velocidad casi nativa casual. Tipos:

- **News reports** — BBC, CNN, Reuters. 2-4 minutos.
- **Interviews largas** — presentador + invitado, 15-30 minutos.
- **Documentales cortos** — TED-Ed, National Geographic Kids, BBC Bitesize.
- **Series con subtítulos EN INGLÉS** — Friends, Modern Family, The Office (US) son ideales por diálogo cotidiano.
- **Movies** (más difícil por acentos y ruido de fondo — considera esto B1+/B2 real).

### 4.2 Estrategias

1. **Predicción por título/contexto.** Antes de escuchar una noticia sobre "elecciones en Alemania", anticipa palabras: *vote, candidate, coalition, chancellor, poll, results*.
2. **Note-taking con abreviaturas.** Aprende un sistema de notas rápido — palabras clave, no frases.
3. **Repetición espaciada del audio.** Primera vez sin subtítulos → segunda vez con subtítulos en inglés → tercera vez sin nada.
4. **Shadowing avanzado.** Repite frases enteras 2 segundos por detrás del audio, imitando entonación y linking.
5. **Transcripción activa (dictado).** Elige un clip de 30 segundos, transcríbelo literal, luego compara con transcripción oficial.

### 4.3 Recursos concretos con URL

- **[BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news)** — noticias con transcripción disponible en muchos casos.
- **[TED Talks](https://www.ted.com/)** — filtra por *"Under 6 minutes"* + *"Beginner-friendly"* para empezar.
- **[TED-Ed](https://ed.ted.com/)** — videos animados de 5 minutos sobre ciencia, historia, tecnología.
- **[The English We Speak (BBC)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/the-english-we-speak)** — 3 min por episodio.
- **[The Daily (New York Times podcast)](https://www.nytimes.com/column/the-daily)** — 20-30 min, inglés americano periodístico.
- **[Serial Podcast](https://serialpodcast.org/)** — narrativa larga, historia por episodios.
- **[This American Life](https://www.thisamericanlife.org/)** — periodismo narrativo, 1 hora por episodio, transcripción en la web.
- **[BBC 6 Minute English](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/6-minute-english)** — nivel intermedio, sigue siendo útil en B1.

### 4.4 Ejercicio ejemplo — anatomía de una noticia BBC

**Fragmento:** *"The European Central Bank has decided to keep interest rates unchanged for the third consecutive month. Analysts had been expecting a rate cut given the recent drop in inflation, but the ECB President said the decision was based on ongoing uncertainty about wage growth."*

**Estructuras gramaticales B1 en esta frase:**
- *has decided* — present perfect (decisión reciente con impacto ahora).
- *had been expecting* — past perfect continuous (expectativa previa a la decisión).
- *given the recent drop* — construcción participial (avanzada).
- *the decision was based on* — passive voice.
- *ongoing uncertainty* — colocación adjective + noun.

**Vocabulario clave a extraer:**
- *interest rates, rate cut, inflation, wage growth, analysts, decision*.

**Este tipo de ejercicio vive en:** `packages/content/src/packs/levels/b1/41-news-current-events.json`.

### 5.4 Storytelling en pasado — dominio de narrative tenses

En B1 debes manejar 3 tiempos combinados en una narrativa:

**Ejemplo (anécdota de viaje, ~90 segundos):**

> *"So last summer, my brother and I decided to hike Kilimanjaro. We had been planning it for over a year — training, saving money, buying gear. When we finally arrived at the base camp in Tanzania, the weather was terrible. It was raining heavily and we were freezing. But we thought, we've come this far, we can't quit now.*
>
> *The first two days went really well. However, on the third night, my brother started feeling sick — altitude sickness. He had been drinking enough water, but his body just wasn't adjusting. Our guide told us we had to descend immediately if we didn't want serious problems.*
>
> *We ended up not reaching the summit, which was really disappointing. But looking back, it was the right decision. His health was more important. We're planning to try again next year, better prepared."*

**Tiempos usados:**
- Past simple: *decided, arrived, was, went, started, told, ended up*.
- Past perfect: *had been planning, had been drinking*.
- Past continuous: *was raining, were freezing, wasn't adjusting*.
- Present perfect: *"we've come this far"*.

### 5.6 Pronunciación B1 — natural rhythm y stress

**Sentence stress:** en inglés se acentúan las palabras de contenido (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) y se reducen las funcionales (articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, pronouns).

Ejemplo: *"I've been WORKING on this PROJECT for THREE WEEKS."*
- Palabras acentuadas (más volumen y duración): WORKING, PROJECT, THREE, WEEKS.
- Palabras reducidas (schwa /ə/): I've, been, on, this, for.

**Contractions obligatorias en hablado B1:**
- *would have* → *would've* (suena "wooda").
- *should have* → *should've* (suena "shooda").
- *could have* → *could've* (suena "cooda").
- *must have* → *must've* (suena "musta").
- *might have* → *might've*.

Nunca escribas *"would of"* — error ortográfico famoso.

**Intonation patterns:**
- Yes/no questions: **rising** al final. *"Are you coming?" ↑*
- Wh- questions: **falling** al final. *"Where are you going?" ↓*
- Statements: **falling**. *"I'm going home." ↓*
- Lists: **rising** en cada ítem, **falling** en el último. *"I bought bread ↑, cheese ↑, and wine ↓."*
- Question tags de confirmación: **falling**. *"It's cold, isn't it?" ↓*
- Question tags de duda real: **rising**. *"You're American, aren't you?" ↑*

## 6. Writing — tipos y estructura en B1

### 6.1 Formal emails (trabajo)

**Estructura estándar:**
1. Subject (claro y específico).
2. Greeting (Dear + Mr./Ms. + apellido, o Dear Sir/Madam).
3. Opening (referencia clara al motivo).
4. Body (2-3 párrafos).
5. Closing (call to action + cortesía).
6. Sign-off (Kind regards / Best regards / Sincerely).

**Ejemplo (~180 palabras):**

```
Subject: Follow-up on our meeting — Next steps for the Q1 campaign

Dear Ms. Chen,

I hope this email finds you well.

I am writing to follow up on our meeting last Tuesday regarding the Q1 marketing campaign. As discussed, I have prepared a draft proposal with the three approaches we agreed to explore.

Please find the document attached. In summary, Option A focuses on social media advertising, Option B combines email marketing with paid search, and Option C is a hybrid strategy. I have included estimated budgets and timelines for each.

Could you please review the proposal by the end of this week? If you have any questions or would like to schedule a follow-up call, please let me know. I would also appreciate any feedback from your team before we present to the client next Monday.

Thank you very much for your time and support on this project. I look forward to hearing from you soon.

Kind regards,
Ana Miranda
Marketing Manager
```

**Anotaciones:**
- Sin contracciones (*"I am"*, no *"I'm"*).
- Cortesía típica: *"I hope this email finds you well"*, *"I would appreciate"*, *"I look forward to hearing from you"*.
- Call to action clara: *"Could you please review… by the end of this week?"*.

**Pack:** `b1/36-work-emails-formal`.

### 6.2 Opinion essay (200-250 palabras)

**Estructura estándar (4 párrafos):**

1. **Introduction** — enunciar tema y opinión clara (thesis statement).
2. **Body paragraph 1** — primera razón + evidencia/ejemplo.
3. **Body paragraph 2** — segunda razón + evidencia/ejemplo (o counterargument + refutación).
4. **Conclusion** — reafirmar opinión con nuevas palabras.

**Ejemplo (~230 palabras):**

> **Prompt:** *Some people believe that governments should provide free university education to all citizens. Do you agree?*
>
> ***Introduction.** The question of whether university education should be free for all citizens is a widely debated topic. In my opinion, free university education would bring significant benefits to society, although it also comes with important challenges.*
>
> ***Body 1.** Firstly, free university would create a fairer society by giving equal opportunities to everyone, regardless of family income. Currently, many talented students from low-income backgrounds cannot afford higher education, which represents a huge loss of potential. For example, countries like Germany and Norway have shown that free tuition can produce highly qualified graduates without excluding anyone based on economic status.*
>
> ***Body 2.** However, we must acknowledge that free university has costs. Taxpayers would have to fund the system, and universities might become overcrowded. Nevertheless, these costs can be justified because a more educated population generates higher tax revenues in the long run, benefiting the entire economy.*
>
> ***Conclusion.** To sum up, while free university education requires public investment, the social and economic benefits clearly outweigh the drawbacks. In my view, governments should treat education as a right rather than a privilege, especially in a knowledge-based economy.*

**Conectores B1 clave para essays:**
- Introducing points: *Firstly, To begin with, First of all*.
- Adding: *Furthermore, In addition, Moreover, What's more*.
- Contrasting: *However, Nevertheless, On the other hand, Although*.
- Giving examples: *For example, For instance, Such as, Namely, To illustrate*.
- Cause/effect: *Therefore, As a result, Consequently, For this reason, Due to*.
- Concluding: *To sum up, In conclusion, All in all, In short, Overall*.

### 6.3 Story (para writing exam)

**Prompt tipo:** *"Write a short story (150-200 words) that begins: 'It was the strangest phone call I had ever received.'"*

**Ejemplo (~180 palabras):**

> *It was the strangest phone call I had ever received. I was working late at the office when my phone rang. The caller ID said "Unknown," but I picked up anyway.*
>
> *"Is this Ana Miranda?" a woman's voice asked. When I confirmed, she said something that made my heart stop: "You need to leave the building immediately. Trust me."*
>
> *At first, I thought it was a joke. However, something in her voice sounded incredibly serious. I grabbed my bag, ran down the stairs and left through the back door. Just as I reached the corner, I heard a loud noise behind me. When I turned around, I saw smoke coming out of the third-floor windows — my floor.*
>
> *The building's electrical system had short-circuited, causing a small fire in the server room. If I had stayed even five more minutes, I could have been trapped.*
>
> *I never found out who called me or how she knew. But every time my phone rings from an unknown number, I answer immediately — just in case.*

**Elementos B1 clave:**
- Past perfect al inicio (*had ever received*).
- Past continuous + past simple (*was working when phone rang*).
- Third conditional emergente (*If I had stayed… I could have been trapped*).
- Reported speech (*She said something that made my heart stop*).
- Phrasal verbs (*picked up, ran down, left through, ran out, turned around*).

## 7. Errores típicos de hispanohablantes en B1

15 errores de nivel específico:

1. **Present perfect vs past simple mal calibrado.** *"I've seen him yesterday"* ❌ → *"I saw him yesterday"* ✅. Regla: si hay tiempo específico terminado, past simple.

2. **Since vs from confundidos.** *"I work here from 2020"* ❌ → *"I've worked here since 2020"* ✅. *Since* con present perfect siempre.

3. **Reported speech sin backshift.** *"He said he is tired"* → correcto en algunos contextos, pero en exam formal usa backshift: *"He said he was tired"*.

4. **Segundo condicional con *would* en el if.** *"If I would have money…"* ❌ → *"If I had money, I would…"* ✅.

5. **Pasiva mal formada.** *"The book was writing by…"* ❌ (progressive-passive mal) → *"The book was written by…"* ✅.

6. **Confusión *make/do* en colocaciones.** *"Make a mistake"* ✅ pero *"make an effort"* ❌ (que sí es correcto) — memoriza las colocaciones fijas.

7. **Say vs tell.** *"He said me that…"* ❌ → *"He told me that…"* ✅. *Say* no acepta objeto persona directo.

8. **Preposiciones con verbos.** *"depend of, listen music, look TV, marry with, arrive to"* — todas erróneas. Correctos: *depend on, listen to, watch TV, marry (sin), arrive at/in*.

9. **Doble negación por interferencia.** *"I don't know nothing"* ❌ → *"I don't know anything"* / *"I know nothing"* ✅.

10. **Third-person -s aún fallando.** Sigue siendo error frecuente en B1 en producción rápida. *"He work"* ❌ → *"He works"* ✅.

11. **Confusión *fun/funny*.** *"It was funny"* solo si algo te hizo REÍR. *"It was fun"* si te lo pasaste bien.

12. **Confusion *good/well*.** *"I speak English good"* ❌ → *"I speak English well"* ✅. *Good* es adjetivo; *well* es adverbio.

13. **Overusing *the*.** *"The love is beautiful"* ❌ → *"Love is beautiful"* ✅. Sustantivos abstractos en sentido general no llevan *the*.

14. **Since as "como" (because).** *"Since it was raining…"* técnicamente correcto pero suena formal/anticuado en B1. Mejor *"Because/As it was raining…"*.

15. **False friends idiomáticos.** *"Realize"* (darse cuenta) confundido con "realizar" (perform). *"Assist"* (asistir a evento) — no; *assist = help*. Refresca la tabla de falsos amigos.

## 8. Plan de estudio semanal (14 semanas típicas)

Plan de 14 semanas × 60 min/día × 6 días = ~84 horas activas. Sumado a exposición pasiva (Netflix, podcast) alcanza las ~150 horas que separan A2+ de B1 consolidado.

### Bloque 1 (semanas 1-4) — Tiempos perfectos

**Semana 1: Present perfect vs past simple**
- Packs: `b1/01-present-perfect-vs-past-simple`.
- Milestone: escribir un párrafo de 100 palabras describiendo tu vida hasta ahora, mezclando ambos tiempos correctamente.

**Semana 2: Present perfect continuous**
- Packs: `b1/02-present-perfect-continuous`.
- Milestone: hablar 2 min sobre "cosas que has estado haciendo últimamente" con 5+ present perfect continuous.

**Semana 3: Past perfect y past perfect continuous**
- Packs: `b1/03-past-perfect`, `b1/04-past-perfect-continuous`.
- Milestone: contar una anécdota con orden temporal claro usando past perfect.

**Semana 4: Narrative tenses combinados**
- Packs: `b1/05-narrative-tenses-combined`, `b1/33-describing-experiences`.
- Milestone: escribir un cuento de 200 palabras con los 4 tiempos del pasado.

### Bloque 2 (semanas 5-7) — Condicionales y modales

**Semana 5: Second conditional + unless/in case/provided**
- Packs: `b1/06-second-conditional`, `b1/07-unless-in-case-provided`.
- Milestone: responder 10 preguntas hipotéticas ("If you could…") con oraciones completas.

**Semana 6: Modales de deducción**
- Packs: `b1/16-modals-deduction-present`, `b1/17-modals-deduction-past`.
- Milestone: describir 5 misterios/situaciones ambiguas con *must have, might have, can't have*.

**Semana 7: Modales de obligación pasada + used to advanced**
- Packs: `b1/18-modals-obligation-past`, `b1/19-used-to-vs-would-vs-be-used-to`.
- Milestone: escribir sobre 5 cosas que "should have done differently" en tu vida pasada.

### Bloque 3 (semanas 8-9) — Pasiva y reported

**Semana 8: Passive voice completa**
- Packs: `b1/08-passive-present-past`, `b1/09-passive-modals`.
- Milestone: escribir un artículo tipo Wikipedia de 150 palabras sobre un evento histórico usando pasiva.

**Semana 9: Reported speech completo**
- Packs: `b1/10-reported-speech-statements`, `b1/11-reported-speech-questions`, `b1/12-reported-commands-requests`.
- Milestone: reportar una conversación real de 10 turnos con backshift correcto.

### Bloque 4 (semanas 10-11) — Relative clauses, gerunds, future

**Semana 10: Relative clauses + gerunds/infinitives**
- Packs: `b1/13-relative-clauses-defining`, `b1/14-relative-clauses-non-defining`, `b1/15-gerunds-vs-infinitives-advanced`.
- Milestone: escribir descripción de personas/lugares con 5 relative clauses.

**Semana 11: Future continuous + future perfect**
- Packs: `b1/20-future-continuous`, `b1/21-future-perfect`.
- Milestone: describir tu vida ideal "en 10 años" usando ambos tiempos.

### Bloque 5 (semanas 12-13) — Vocabulario avanzado y funciones sociales

**Semana 12: Phrasal verbs + idioms + collocations**
- Packs: `b1/22-phrasal-verbs-b1-communication`, `b1/23-phrasal-verbs-b1-business`, `b1/24-phrasal-verbs-b1-travel`, `b1/25-idioms-common-b1`, `b1/26-collocations-verb-noun`, `b1/27-collocations-adj-noun`.
- Milestone: usar 15 phrasal verbs y 5 idioms en una conversación de 5 min.

**Semana 13: Opinar, acordar, quejar, explicar**
- Packs: `b1/28-expressing-preferences`, `b1/29-expressing-agreement-shades`, `b1/30-expressing-disagreement-polite`, `b1/31-making-complaints`, `b1/32-giving-explanations`, `b1/48-giving-advice-b1`.
- Milestone: participar en un debate de 10 min sobre "trabajo remoto vs oficina" con 3 fórmulas distintas de opinar y acordar/disentir.

### Bloque 6 (semana 14) — Aplicación profesional + evaluación

**Semana 14: Trabajo, entrevistas, temas actuales**
- Packs: `b1/34-talking-about-changes`, `b1/35-work-meetings-b1`, `b1/36-work-emails-formal`, `b1/37-work-phone-calls`, `b1/38-presentations-basic`, `b1/39-job-interviews-basic`, `b1/40-cv-linkedin-basic`, `b1/41-news-current-events`, `b1/50-b1-review-mixed`.
- Test final: sample de Cambridge B1 Preliminary — apuntar a 70% (pass oficial). Ver [sample](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/preliminary/preparation/).

### Cuándo pasar al siguiente nivel — Checklist para B2

Marca las 8 antes de saltar a B2:

- ✅ Aprobé (o aprobaría) B1 Preliminary con 70%+ en simulacro.
- ✅ Distingo present perfect vs past simple con <10% error en escritura.
- ✅ Formo segundo condicional automáticamente sin pensar.
- ✅ Manejo reported speech con backshift correcto en 80%+ de casos.
- ✅ Puedo entender un episodio completo de TED-Ed sin subtítulos.
- ✅ Escribo un email formal de 200 palabras sin buscar más de 3-4 palabras.
- ✅ Uso 30+ phrasal verbs y 15+ idioms activamente.
- ✅ Manejo activamente 2.500-3.500 palabras (mide con [Test Your Vocabulary](https://testyourvocab.com/)).

Si marcaste 6-8 → adelante con B2. Si menos, dedica 3-4 semanas más consolidando.
